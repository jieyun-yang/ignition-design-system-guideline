# Illustrations
```markdown
## Overview

Illustrations are a foundational design element used to quickly communicate information, influence mood, and enhance the overall user experience within the product. This guideline outlines the different types of illustrations, core principles for their effective use, and specific product contexts where they can be applied, particularly in empty states.

## Usage Guidelines

Illustrations should be used thoughtfully to support the user experience and convey specific messages or emotions.

### When to Use Illustrations

*   To quickly communicate information.
*   To influence the user's mood or emotional state.
*   To support the user rather than hinder them.

### When to Consider Illustration Types

Before incorporating an illustration, determine the most appropriate type:
*   **Icon**: For simple, symbolic representation. (Inferred from principles)
*   **Spot Illustration**: For focused, smaller visual accents.
*   **Scene Illustration**: For richer, more contextual visual narratives.

### Product Usage Contexts

#### Empty States

Illustrations are commonly used in empty states to provide context, guide users, or convey a specific emotional tone when no content is present. The emotional tone of the illustration should align with the message and user's potential sentiment.

*   **Neutral**: The default and most common use case. Used when the message is communicated factually, e.g., "No draft proposals."
*   **Positive**: Used when the lack of content signifies a positive outcome or a desired state, e.g., "No outstanding notifications/tasks," "No overdue manual billing."
*   **(Slightly) Negative**: Used when the absence of content is not the ideal state, encouraging action, e.g., "No active or lead clients."
*   **Very Positive**: Reserved for significant positive events or celebrations.

Illustrations within the design system are categorized by their complexity and purpose.

### Spot Illustrations

These are typically smaller, focused illustrations used to highlight a specific element or concept without needing extensive contextual background.

### Scene Illustrations

These are more complex illustrations that depict a broader narrative or context, often involving characters, environments, or multiple elements to tell a story or set a mood.

## Best Practices

Adhere to these principles for effective and impactful use of illustrations.

*   **Use Carefully**: Illustrations are powerful tools for communication and mood setting. However, overuse can be distracting and diminish their overall value. Always evaluate whether an illustration is truly appropriate for the context.
*   **Determine Type**: Before including an illustration, consider whether an icon, spot illustration, or scene illustration is the most suitable type for the message.
*   **Avoid Redundancy**: Do not use the same illustration in different, unrelated contexts (e.g., an empty state illustration should not be reused for onboarding).
*   **Focus on the Message**: Illustrations must support and clarify the user's understanding, not hinder it. Ensure the chosen illustration clearly conveys the intended message.
*   **Prioritize Quality**: Avoid using generic "library illustrations" if they only do an "okay" job of communicating your message. We can always create more bespoke illustrations if necessary to achieve a "great" outcome.
*   **Design for Emotion**: Think critically about the emotional reaction you want to elicit from the user. Adjust the illustration's style and content to match the user's likely mood (e.g., satisfaction after completing a large task vs. frustration encountering an error).
```
