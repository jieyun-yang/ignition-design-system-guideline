# Grammar
## Overview

This guideline outlines the essential grammar and writing style rules for all product content and communications. Adhering to these standards ensures consistency, clarity, and a professional voice across all our platforms, providing a unified and intuitive experience for users. It covers capitalization, spelling, punctuation, date and time formats, number usage, and more.

## Usage Guidelines

### Bold Text
Use bold formatting for specific elements to draw attention or indicate interactivity:
*   **Key numbers or data**: Such as transaction numbers, card or bank account numbers, or dollar values.
*   **Name of UI elements in product tours**: Such as button names or navigational items.
*   **Terms or phrases that need emphasis**: Particularly in bulleted lists with run-in statements.

### Dash & Hyphen Usage

Understand the correct use of hyphens, en dashes, and em dashes.

| Type of dash | Use for:                                                                                                                               |
| :----------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hyphen (-)   | Joining 2 or more words together (e.g., client-specific). <br> Prefixes, when the prefix ends with a vowel and the following word starts with a vowel (e.g., pre-existing, re-enter), except when commonly spelled without a hyphen (e.g., cooperation). <br> Blank fields in table data. |
| En dash (–)  | Ranges or numbers (e.g., 2023–2024, Monday–Friday, Melbourne–Sydney).                                                                  |
| Em dash (—)  | Use sparingly. It can be used for adding extra information to a sentence that's not essential to its meaning—like a side note (e.g., "Your demo proposal will come from Growth Accounting—that's us."). |

### Personal Pronouns

Use pronouns consistently to maintain a clear and helpful voice.

| Pronoun  | Rule                                                                                             | Example                                             |
| :------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------- |
| You, your| Use when asking questions, giving instructions, or describing something.                          | You'll get an email when your client accepts the proposal |
| I, my    | Use when the user is making a choice or performing an action.                                   | I accept the terms and conditions of my agreement   |
| We, us   | Use "we" to refer to Ignition as an entity. Use when we're taking responsibility for something or acting on behalf of a user. | There was a problem on our end, but we're working on fixing it.<br>Tell us about your company |

## Best Practices

### Capitalization

#### Sentence Case
*   **Do**: Always use sentence case for headings, labels, and general text. This means only the first word of a sentence or phrase, and any proper nouns, are capitalized.
    *   **Do** | **Don't**
    *   Create proposal | Create Proposal
    *   Are you sure you want to delete Space Ranger? | Are You Sure You Want to Delete Space Ranger?
    *   Unable to send proposal | Unable To Send Proposal
    *   50 clients selected | 50 Clients selected

#### Proper Nouns and Acronyms
*   **Do**: Capitalize proper nouns and acronyms correctly.
    *   **Do** | **Don't**
    *   John Smith | john smith
    *   Bacs | BACS
    *   GST | gst
    *   Services library | Services Library
    *   QuickBooks | Quickbooks

### American Spelling
*   **Do**: Use American spelling conventions.
    *   **Do** | **Don't**
    *   Apologize | Apologise
    *   Customize | Customise
    *   Color | Colour
    *   Center | Centre
    *   Favorite | Favourite
    *   Installment | Instalment
    *   Organization | Organisation
    *   Canceled | Cancelled

### Bulleted Lists
*   **Do**: Use end punctuation for complete sentences in list items.
*   **Don't**: Add end punctuation if the item consists of a single word.
*   **Don't**: Add end punctuation if the item is a link.
    *   **Do** | **Don't**
    *   Upgrade your plan and you’ll also:
        *   Save time with custom proposal templates.
        *   Automate workflows by integrating your practice management system. | Upgrade your plan and you’ll also:
                                                                            *   save time with custom proposal templates
                                                                            *   automate workflows by integrating your practice management system
    *   Supported credit cards in your region:
        *   Visa
        *   MasterCard
        *   American Express | Supported credit cards in your region
                               *   Visa.
                               *   MasterCard.
                               *   American Express.
*   **Example for complete sentences**: Collect payments automatically. Get paid on time.

### Periods (Full Stops)
*   **Do**: Leave the full stop off if it's not a complete sentence.
*   **Don't**: Use a full stop for single short sentences.

### Numbers
*   **Do**: Use commas for numbers with four or more digits (e.g., 4,000).
*   **Do**: When specifying currency with a dollar value, the currency comes after the dollar amount (e.g., $400 USD).

### Number Truncation
*   **Do**: Add a dollar sign to indicate the amount is in dollars (e.g., $5K).
*   **Don't**: Put a space between the number and K (thousands) or M (millions) (e.g., 5K, not 5 K).
