# Our voice
```markdown
## Overview
This guideline defines the fundamental principles of our brand's voice and personality, ensuring consistent and effective communication across all platforms and touchpoints. It covers our distinct voice and tone, brand personality, guidelines for language regionalization, and best practices for inclusive language across various contexts. Adhering to these guidelines helps us create clear, engaging, and respectful content for all our users.

## Usage Guidelines
These guidelines should be applied to all user-facing content, internal communications, marketing materials, product interfaces, and any written or spoken communication representing our brand.
- **Voice & Tone:** Consistently apply the defined voice and tone (Straight up, Charismatic, Resourceful) to reflect our brand's personality in all communications.
- **Language & Regionalization:** Prioritize regionalized language where appropriate to resonate with local audiences, especially for terms with significant regional variants.
- **Inclusive Language:** Always use inclusive language to ensure our content is welcoming, respectful, and accessible to everyone, avoiding terms that could be seen as exclusive, biased, or outdated.

## Best Practices

### Voice & Tone
Our voice is built on three core pillars:

#### Straight up
Communicate clearly and directly, without unnecessary jargon or overly complex phrasing. Be honest and transparent in your messaging.

#### Charismatic
Infuse warmth, energy, and a positive outlook into your writing. Be engaging and approachable, making interactions feel personable and inviting.

#### Resourceful
Provide helpful, practical, and solution-oriented information. Empower users with knowledge and guidance that supports their goals.

## Language & Regionalization
Recognize and adapt to regional language differences to ensure content is culturally appropriate and easily understood by local audiences. When significant regional variations exist, regionalize the content; otherwise, follow the preferred approach.

### Examples of regional language differences

| Concept/Term | Regional Variants (AU/UK/NZ) | Regional Variants (US/CA) | Preferred Approach |
```
