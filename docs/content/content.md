# CONTENT
## Overview

This guideline outlines the core principles for creating effective and user-centric content within the design system. It focuses on ensuring clarity, usefulness, consistency, and building user confidence across all textual elements of the product. Adhering to these guidelines helps create a cohesive and empowering user experience.

## Usage Guidelines

These content principles and best practices should be applied whenever crafting text for the product, including but not limited to:

*   Page and section titles (Headings)
*   Calls to action (Buttons)
*   System feedback messages (Error messages, Toasts)
*   Overlay titles and labelling (Modals)
*   Data formats (Table data)
*   Feature names

## Best Practices

### Principles of Content

These principles guide the creation of all content within the design system.

#### Use plain language

**Description:** Keep things simple and easy to understand. Avoid technical jargon, industry or region-specific language unless the content is specifically targeted.

**Do:**
*   Improve clarity and comprehension by explaining things in simple terms.
*   Use active verbs instead of nominalizations and break up convoluted noun strings with particles.

**Don't:**
*   Avoid technical language, especially if it doesn't match up to the UI.

#### Get to the point

**Description:** People only read what’s valuable and relevant to them. Deliver your message in short, scannable chunks, focusing on just a few ideas at once.

**Do:**
*   Be concise. Communicate the important points and cut out filler words.
*   Show copy that relates to the user at the time that they need it.
*   Copy should help the user complete their desired goal or action, and prevent mistakes.

**Don't:**
*   Don't write instructions that are longer than necessary.
*   Skip the "Are you sure?".
*   Use contextual headings to avoid repeating yourself in the body or buttons.
*   If information doesn't relate to the user, don't show it.
*   If the information has no impact on the decision the user is making, consider whether you need to show it at the particular moment.

#### Be useful

**Description:** Write copy that helps users do what they're trying to do. Consider what info they need to confidently move forward and avoid unwanted consequences at the given moment. If it's not relevant, don't show it.

*See "Get to the point" for additional specific Do's and Don'ts that also apply to usefulness.*

#### Be consistent

**Description:** Look for and use patterns. Use the same terminology for functions and elements appearing in multiple locations in the app.

**Do:**
*   Use consistent terminology throughout the app.

**Don't:**
*   Don't use different terminology for the same thing.

#### Instil confidence and control

**Description:** We want users to feel confident and in control of their business. Keep the language positive and empowering, deliver bad news gently and provide next steps where possible.

**Do:**
*   Clearly communicate what went wrong.
*   Provide reassurance where necessary and a resolution to help the user rectify the issue.
*   Messages could do with a more encouraging tone.

**Don't:**
*   Don't blame the user or make them feel like they did something wrong.
*   Try to focus on what they can do to rectify the issue.

## Accessibility

Following these content guidelines, especially "Use plain language" and "Get to the point," contributes significantly to the accessibility of the product. By ensuring content is easily understandable, concise, and free of jargon, the design system makes information more accessible to a wider range of users, including those with cognitive differences, non-native speakers, or those using assistive technologies.
